Treating a Portfolio Like a Small Product

Technical Writeup · 2026

The challenge. Building a personal portfolio is not only a visual design problem. The challenge was to create a site that could communicate technical work, research interests, and long-term growth clearly without becoming visually heavy or difficult to navigate.

My approach. I approached the portfolio as a small product rather than a collection of pages. I explored a component-based HTML structure, a token-driven CSS system, responsive layouts, semantic markup, and a Git workflow that keeps design and implementation decisions traceable.

Outcome. The result is a single-page site with no external framework, built section by section, with each section committed to Git on its own so every design and implementation decision stays traceable in the project's history.

Why it matters. The same principles apply to larger systems I want to build: break a problem into components, make decisions explicit and versioned, and treat maintainability as a requirement from the start, not an afterthought.

About

How I think about building things, and why I got here.

It started when my sister was learning to code for a university course, and her explaining HTML and CSS to me turned a long-standing curiosity about technology into something I could actually build. The more I learned, the more I noticed something I didn't expect: how much code reflected the way I already thought about life.

To me, the frontend is like a person's face — one visible flaw, and it's all anyone notices. The backend is like habits: the quiet structure that makes everything else run smoothly. And bugs are just like problems in life — best solved with calm and awareness, not panic.

For years, I researched everything and focused on nothing. Constantly questioning myself eventually taught me that focus and planning mattered more than knowing a little about everything. That's how I built the framework I still use today: not a rigid schedule, but a way of managing my life rather than controlling it.

I've also spent a lot of time studying psychology — partly to work through negative self-talk and low self-confidence, and partly to build deeper relationships with the people around me. Football has been part of that same framework. It keeps both my mind and body sharp, and reinforces the same discipline I try to bring to everything else — including the projects below.
